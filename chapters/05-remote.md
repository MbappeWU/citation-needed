<a id="remote-collaboration"></a>
### Remote work damages collaboration.

**~ It depends · Grade B** (Two large observational datasets agree that communication changes under full remote work. Neither is randomised, and both come from pandemic conditions.)

**What the research says.** After Microsoft moved to firm-wide remote work in 2020, an analysis of 61,182 US employees' emails, calendars, chats and calls (December 2019 to June 2020) found a more static, siloed collaboration network with fewer bridges between groups, less live communication and more asynchronous communication. At an Asian IT-services firm, time in meetings and coordination rose, uninterrupted hours shrank and staff networked with fewer people and units. At Trip.com, two days a week at home did not change performance reviews.

**Limits.** Microsoft's shift was sudden, firm-wide and mid-pandemic, with no control group, and it measured network structure, not project quality. Neither study separates remote work from lockdown. Two days a week at home (Trip.com) does not extend to fully remote teams.

> **Say this to your boss:** At Microsoft, going fully remote left collaboration more siloed, with fewer links between groups. A Trip.com trial found two days a week at home did not hurt performance. If we go remote, let's plan cross-team contact on purpose.

**What would change my mind.** A randomised comparison of fully remote and co-located software teams showing no change in cross-team ties or delivery outcomes, or a randomised hybrid trial showing weaker collaboration.

<a id="remote-developers-output"></a>
### Remote developers produce less.

**~ It depends · Grade C** (Developer-specific evidence is thin and points both ways: one trial of hybrid work, one observational case study at Baidu and one pandemic study of IT staff.)

**What the research says.** In the Trip.com randomised trial, software engineers at home two days a week wrote no fewer lines of code than office colleagues. At Baidu, a study of 139 developers over 138 working days (1,103 records from home, during the pandemic) found gains and losses depending on the metric: builds, commits and code reviews. Large projects fared worse. At an Asian IT-services firm with 10,000+ staff, output per hour fell 8 to 19% in the pandemic as hours rose.

**Limits.** Lines of code, commits and builds are crude proxies for output. The Baidu data are one firm's observational records from the pandemic, and the IT-services data cover a pandemic switch. None of the studies cited randomised full-time remote work for developers.

> **Say this to your boss:** For developers the evidence is mixed. Engineers at home two days a week wrote as much code in a Chinese trial, while a pandemic study found lower output per hour. Let's compare our own delivery metrics before changing the policy.

**What would change my mind.** A randomised trial of full-time remote work for developers, using delivery measures beyond code volume, that shows a consistent gain or loss.

2. Lingfeng Bao, Tao Li, Xin Xia, Kaiyu Zhu, Hui Li, Xiaohu Yang (2022). How does working from home affect developer productivity? A case study of Baidu during the COVID-19 pandemic. <a id="remote-hybrid-performance"></a>
### Hybrid work hurts performance and promotion.

**✗ Not supported · Grade B** (One large randomised trial (1,612 employees) with two years of follow-up, but run at a single firm and testing only two days a week at home.)

**What the research says.** Trip.com, a Chinese travel-tech firm, randomised 1,612 university-graduate employees, including software engineers, by birthday: home on Wednesday and Friday, or in the office five days. The trial ran six months in 2021 to 2022, with reviews and promotions tracked for up to two years. Performance grades did not differ, there was no detectable difference in promotions, and engineers' lines of code did not differ. Quit rates fell by about one-third. The trial's 395 managers had expected hybrid to cut productivity by 2.6% on average, and afterwards expected a 1.0% gain. In the earlier Ctrip call-centre trial, full-time home workers had a lower promotion rate given their performance.

**Limits.** One firm, one country, and graduate employees in four functions. It tested two days a week, so it says nothing about full-time remote work, where the Ctrip trial saw a promotion penalty. Careers beyond two years are unmeasured.

> **Say this to your boss:** A six-month randomised trial at Trip.com found that two days a week at home did not change performance reviews or promotions over two years, and cut quit rates by a third. We can pilot the same schedule and track the same measures.

**What would change my mind.** A second randomised hybrid trial at another firm, ideally with software teams and a longer follow-up, showing lower ratings or fewer promotions for people who work from home.

**Sources** (checked 2026-10-01):

<a id="remote-juniors-learning"></a>
### Junior engineers cannot learn properly when working remotely.

**~ It depends · Grade B** (One careful study with two natural experiments at a large firm, backed by an observational IT-services study, but feedback is an indirect measure of learning.)

**What the research says.** At a Fortune 500 firm (2019 to 2024), software engineers who sat near their teammates received 18.3% more feedback on their code, and their code quality was higher. The study used the 2020 office closures and the 2022 to 2023 return-to-office mandates. The gains were concentrated among younger and less-tenured engineers. Experienced engineers wrote less code when sitting near teammates, so proximity trades their output for juniors' feedback. At an Asian IT-services firm, remote staff got less coaching and fewer 1:1 meetings with supervisors.

**Limits.** One firm, and feedback and code quality are indirect measures of learning. The study compares teams that were close or far apart, and it does not test fixes such as scheduled mentoring or in-person onboarding.

> **Say this to your boss:** At one large firm, engineers sitting near teammates got 18.3% more code feedback, with the gain concentrated among juniors. If juniors work remotely, let's schedule reviews and pairing for them on purpose.

**What would change my mind.** Long-run data showing that juniors on remote or hybrid teams build skills and get promoted at the same pace as co-located peers, or a randomised mentoring trial with software teams.

**Sources** (checked 2026-10-01):

1. Natalia Emanuel, Emma Harrington, Amanda Pallais (2026). The Power of Proximity to Coworkers. The Quarterly Journal of Economics 141(3). [link](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjag027) <sub>✓ metadata checked (title, authors, year)</sub> (primary)

<a id="remote-video-creativity"></a>
### Video calls are as good as meeting in person for creative work.

**✗ Not supported · Grade B** (Randomised lab and field experiments agree, and an observational study points the same way, but the experiments test short idea-generation tasks.)

**What the research says.** Brucks and Levav randomised pairs to brainstorm on video or in the same room, in a lab study (602 people) and a field experiment with 1,490 engineers at a multinational firm in five countries. Pairs on video generated fewer ideas, and fewer creative ones. The authors link this to the narrower visual focus on a screen. When pairs chose which idea to pursue, video groups did no worse. A separate study of 20 million papers and 4 million patents found that teams spread across locations were less likely to produce breakthroughs.

**Limits.** The tasks were short pair brainstorms, not weeks of design or coding. The field participants were engineers at one firm. The study cannot say whether longer sessions or better tools close the gap. The papers and patents study compares teams in different locations, not video calls.

> **Say this to your boss:** In randomised experiments, pairs on video produced fewer ideas, and fewer creative ones, than pairs in the same room, but chose between ideas just as well. Let's brainstorm in person when we can and use video for deciding and follow-up.

**What would change my mind.** Independent replications with software teams showing equal idea counts and creativity on video in longer sessions, or a video format that closes the gap.

**Sources** (checked 2026-10-01):

1. Melanie S. Brucks, Jonathan Levav (2022). Virtual communication curbs creative idea generation. Nature 605. [link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04643-y) <sub>✓ metadata checked (title, authors, year)</sub> (primary)
2. Yiling Lin, Carl Benedikt Frey, Lingfei Wu (2023). Remote collaboration fuses fewer breakthrough ideas. Nature 623. [link](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06767-1) <sub>✓✓ metadata and quoted numbers checked against the abstract</sub> (context)

<a id="remote-wfh-productivity"></a>
### Working from home lowers productivity.

**~ It depends · Grade B** (Two randomised trials at one Chinese company found a gain or no loss, but they covered a call centre and two days a week at home. A pandemic-era study of IT staff found output per hour fell.)

**What the research says.** At Ctrip (now Trip.com), call-centre staff who volunteered were randomly assigned to work from home for 9 months. Their performance rose 13%, mostly from more minutes worked, and attrition halved. Once the firm let staff choose, the gain rose to 22%. In a later Trip.com trial of 1,612 university-graduate employees, including software engineers, two days a week at home left performance reviews and engineers' lines of code unchanged. Against that, 10,000+ staff at an Asian IT-services firm worked more hours in the pandemic, output fell slightly, and output per hour dropped 8 to 19%.

**Limits.** Both trials ran at the same company, and the first was call-centre work with volunteers. The second tested only two days a week at home. The IT-services study was not randomised and covers the pandemic switch to working from home.

> **Say this to your boss:** Randomised trials at a Chinese company found a gain for call-centre volunteers working from home and no loss for staff at home two days a week. A pandemic study of IT staff found output per hour fell. Let's measure our own output per hour before deciding.

**What would change my mind.** A randomised trial of full-time remote work with software teams, run outside a lockdown, showing a clear drop in output per hour. Or the Trip.com results failing to repeat at other firms.
